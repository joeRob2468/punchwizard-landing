# Design

How the site works. The rationale and roadmap are in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md); this file describes what exists now.

## Page structure

`src/main.tsx` checks for WebGL 2 and mounts either `<App>` or `<Fallback>`.

`<App>` (`src/app/App.tsx`) stacks two layers in one full-viewport `<main>`:

1. **Scene** (`<Canvas>`): camera rig, the specimen (`specimen/Specimen`, inside `<Suspense>` while it loads) and the post-processing stack. The renderer runs with no tone mapping (`flat`) so palette colours stay exact, DPR capped at 2 (see § Resolution).
2. **Overlay**: DOM above the canvas, `pointer-events: none` by default. Holds the printed double rule (`ui/PlateBorder`), the title block (`ui/PlateHeader`) and, while the specimen loads, `ui/Preloader`: a ring of the 24 Futhark runes that ink in clockwise as parts arrive (`loadSpecimen`'s progress, through the store's `loadProgress`), with the caption _Praeparatur specimen_. It fades out once `Specimen` has drawn its first frame (`specimenReady`).

`<Fallback>` shows the same border and title on CSS paper (`ui/PaperBackdrop`), with a still of the specimen in its resting cut (`public/fallback/plate.webp`, JPEG for older browsers; captured from the live plate with the overlay hidden, edges feathered by a CSS mask) and a note that the living plate needs WebGL 2. `<noscript>` in `index.html` shows the same still. It's used when WebGL 2 is missing and, through `ui/ErrorBoundary`, when the plate fails to render (for example, the specimen can't be fetched).

`index.html` carries the title, description, canonical URL and link-preview tags (Open Graph and X/Twitter `summary_large_image`). The preview image, `public/og-image.jpg`, is a 1200 × 630 title card: _Pugnomagus pileatus_ (IM Fell English italic, 112 px) over _The Common Punchwizard_ (small caps, 50 px) on dark paper, behind them a faint ring of the Futhark runes (`ornament/futhark.ts`) and rune bands inside a double rule. It was drawn as a throwaway page in the dev server (so it could use the project font and rune paths), captured at 2× and downscaled. Preview tags need absolute URLs, so these name the live domain. Icons are in `public/` too: `favicon.svg` (the hat on dark paper, outlined in chalk) is the source; `favicon.ico` (16/32/48), `icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png` are renders of it, and `apple-touch-icon.png` (180) and `icon-maskable-512.png` are full-bleed versions with the hat scaled into the platforms' safe zone. `site.webmanifest` lists them.

In dev, `scene/DevHandle` exposes the R3F state getter as `window.__plate` for inspection.

## Procedural specimen

Everything the wizard is made of is generated in `src/procedural/` (pure TypeScript, no DOM) and meshed ahead of time by the **bake**.

### Space and landmarks

Specimen space: origin near the centre of the head, y up, the face looking along +z, +x on the wizard's left; one unit is about a head's height. `landmarks.ts` names the reference points (eyes, nose tip, chin, ears, neck, shoulders, brim centre, hat tip, beard tip, bust base). Parts are positioned from them, and label anchors will refer to them.

### Parts

Each part (`parts/*.ts`) is a `PartDefinition`: an id, a layer, a look (palette key, optional glow, section pattern), a signed distance field, the bounds the surface must fit in, a cell size for meshing, and optionally `intendedHoles` (through-holes it is meant to have) and `gazePivot` (turns to follow the pointer). `specimen.ts` lists the parts **outermost to innermost**, which is also the order section caps are drawn in (see Sectioning). Names and lore are in [anatomy.md](anatomy.md).

### The horn core

The head doesn't stop at the brim: `parts/cranium.ts` grows a horn core up into the hat, each layer the hat's own crown (`parts/hat-shape.ts`: spine, radius, crumples and creases) shrunk by an inset: skin, bone and cavity (`HORN`). The head, skull and cranial cavity union their usual shapes with it, so the brain fills it (the Pileal Lobe), and the hat keeps an even felt wall around it. The Third Fontanelle opens at the cavity's tip on the midline (`hornTip`), where the Conduit Lattice leaves for the hat's point.

### Air spaces

`parts/airways.ts` defines the nasal cavity (a profile outline from the aperture to the choanae, narrow under the cribriform plate and wide over the palate, with the septum and conchae), the frontal sinus, and the pharynx, larynx and trachea along `AIRWAY_PATH`. Each tissue carves them differently so no two parts share a surface (shared surfaces flicker): bone carves them grown by the mucosa's thickness (`boneAirway`), the head carves the true air and wraps the septum and conchae in mucosa (`mucosaAirway`), and robe, mantle and torso carve them grown a little more (`tissueAirway`). Outer parts that would otherwise reach inside the head (the hat's brim and cone base, the hair cap, the beard core) are kept outside the head's skin field, and the skull is kept at least 12 mm inside it, so nothing shows through in a section or pokes out of the face. The cranial vault and cavity live in `parts/cranium.ts`, shared by skull, brain and head.

### Growth

`growth/locks.ts` grows hair: a lock is a chain of tapered segments from a root, pulled by gravity, an optional drift and target, with a sideways wave and a curl toward the tip, pushed out of collider fields (head, robe, hat) so it slides over them. `growClump` adds thinner strands around a guide lock that gather toward its tip, which reads as a sculpted lock with grooves once blended. Hair parts take their bounds from the grown segments (`segmentsBounds`). Gaps between locks are real through-holes, so the bake's hole warnings for hair parts are expected.

`growth/branches.ts` builds tubes as tapered segments: `curve` makes a trunk along a quadratic Bézier, and `sprout` grows seeded random side branches (with lean, a directional bias and recursive depth) from existing segments. `segmentsField` turns a list into one smooth SDF. It buckets segments in a 0.1-unit grid so each sample only tests nearby ones; where none are near, it returns the bucket size, a safe lower bound. `growth/random.ts` is a seeded PRNG (mulberry32).

`intersectLazy(shape, container, skipBeyond)` skips the expensive container (noisy skin) where the cheap shape is already far away.

### SDF toolkit

`sdf/` has primitives (after Inigo Quilez's distance functions), hard and smooth booleans, offsets, shells, rotations, mirroring, displacement, domain warps and seeded value noise (`fbm`). Fields take three numbers and return one, so sampling doesn't allocate.

### Meshing

`mesh/surface-nets.ts` meshes a field on a regular grid:

1. Sample the field in 4×4×4 blocks; a block whose centre is far from the surface (beyond twice its half-diagonal) takes the centre's value instead of sampling every point.
2. One vertex per cell the surface crosses, at the average of its edge crossings.
3. One quad per grid edge with a sign change, joining the four cells around it, wound so the normal points outward.
4. Each vertex is pulled onto the surface with Newton steps; normals come from the field's gradient.

Meshes are closed as long as the surface stays inside the part's bounds; the mesher throws `SurfaceOutOfBoundsError` if it reaches the grid edge. Features thinner than about three cells can pinch (an edge shared by four triangles); keep thin parts (brim, hat tip) at least that thick.

### Bake and format

`scripts/bake.ts` meshes parts in parallel worker threads (`scripts/bake-worker.ts`). Per part:

1. Mesh the field (surface nets, above) and drop disconnected pieces under 60 triangles (noise islands; `mesh/components.ts`).
2. Count through-holes from the Euler characteristic (`mesh/topology.ts`) and warn if there are more than the part's `intendedHoles` (the skull declares 4). A tunnel keeps a mesh closed, so open-edge checks miss it, but it shows on screen as a hole; typical causes are two shapes touching in two places with a gap between, or a carve or noise deeper than a thin feature.
3. Simplify with meshoptimizer to a fifth of the cell size in error, weighting normals so shading curvature survives (`scripts/simplify.ts`, bake-only so the WebAssembly never reaches the browser). Surviving vertices keep their exact positions and SDF normals.
4. Re-orient any triangle facing inward, using the SDF normals at its corners (`mesh/orient.ts`); backward triangles are culled and read as holes.
5. Bake ambient occlusion per vertex (`mesh/occlusion.ts`): outer (integument) parts from all outer parts' fields together, bones from the skeleton's together (eye sockets, the nasal aperture, between the ribs).

Output is `generated/specimen/` (git-ignored): `manifest.json` (format version 4: parts, layers, looks, counts, bounds, gaze pivots, landmarks) and one `<part>.bin` each (`format/specimen-format.ts`). `scripts/encode-part.ts` reorders each mesh for the vertex cache, interleaves its vertices (12 bytes: positions quantised to Int16 within the part's bounds, normalised Int8 normals, Uint8 occlusion) and compresses vertices and indices with meshoptimizer's codec, which with gzip roughly halves the download. Output goes to a temporary folder that replaces the old one at the end.

`vite-plugins/bake.ts` runs the bake in a child process when the dev server starts and whenever a file in `src/procedural/` changes, serves `generated/specimen/` at `/specimen/`, invalidates the changed modules (the page imports some of them) and reloads the page when the bake finishes. In builds it emits the files into `dist/specimen/` and adds a `<link rel="preload">` for each to `index.html`, so the specimen downloads alongside the script rather than after it; `npm run build` bakes first.

Current output: about 400k triangles in 51 parts, 2.2 MB (2.0 MB gzipped), baked in about 90 s (head, hair and beard dominate).

### Loading

`specimen/load-specimen.ts` fetches the manifest and buffers (started at module load, so it overlaps start-up), decompresses them with meshoptimizer's decoder (a few KB of WebAssembly) and builds a `BufferGeometry` per part with a bounding sphere from the part's bounds. Production builds turn off three.js's shader error checks (`gl.debug.checkShaderErrors`), which made start-up wait on each compile. `specimen/Specimen.tsx` creates an `InkWashMaterial` per part from its look and advances material time each frame.

## Render pipeline

The scene is drawn as **light on black** (the scene background is black), then post-processed:

1. **Surface: `InkWashMaterial`** (`rendering/materials/`, shaders in `rendering/shaders/ink-wash.*.glsl`). A `ShaderMaterial` with clipping enabled.
   - Lit like a photograph, against lights fixed in **view space**, so the specimen is always lit the same way whichever way it's turned: a wrapped key light from the upper left, a cool fill from the lower right and a rim from behind, darkened by the baked occlusion attribute (specimen parts set `useOcclusion`).
   - Each part is made of a **surface** (`config/surfaces.ts`, chosen by part id or wash: skin, cloth, leather, hair, bone, wet tissue, gilt metal) that sets its highlight (strength, tightness, metallic tint, broken up by noise), sheen at grazing angles, warm light scattered under it at the terminator, a fine relief bumped into the normal from screen-space derivatives of a noise height (pores, nap; stretched along y into strands for hair; each octave faded where it would be finer than a pixel, or it shimmers as the model turns) and how much its colour varies in brightness and hue. This is what keeps the specimen from reading as smooth plastic.
   - `glow` (0–1) makes a part self-lit: pale centre, saturated rim, slow pulse. Used for arcane parts.
2. **`OutlineEffect`** (`rendering/effects/`): reads the depth buffer and the part ids in alpha. A clearly farther neighbour draws a silhouette on the near side of the jump; spikes in depth curvature draw fainter creases; a change of part id draws a part edge (see below). Sample positions wobble and line width varies with screen-space noise.

   Depth and part ids have one sample per pixel, so sub-pixel slivers (thin edges at grazing angles, stray cap samples) appear in depth or alpha as single odd pixels. To keep them from being inked as flickering specks, a difference only counts if it persists two steps out in the same direction, and a pixel nearer than at least 7 of its 8 neighbours is never inked.

3. **`PaperEffect`**: screens the result onto dark procedural paper fixed to the screen (mottling, fibres, foxing, vignette, at the view's texture strength). Grain noise modulates the marks (granulation). Empty areas (black) become plain paper.

**Part edges.** Surface and cap materials write a per-part id (manifest index + 1; `specimen/part-id.ts`) to the alpha channel, and the outline effect also inks wherever that id changes between neighbouring pixels. A part whose look sets `inkAs` writes another part's id instead, so one tissue split across two parts (the torso continuing the head's flesh below the neck) shows no seam. This outlines each tissue on a cut face, where caps are coplanar and depth alone shows nothing, and separates overlapping parts. Helpers that blend (the plane's guide lines) leave alpha untouched.

Both effects use `BlendFunction.SET` and run in one merged pass, outline first. Shared noise functions are in `shaders/lib/noise.glsl` (prefixed `pw`).

Tunables (light direction, outline width and strength, part-edge strength) are in `src/config/render.ts`.

### Spotlight

In the Tabula view the specimen is lit like a stage: a warm spotlight in the scene (`specimen/SpotRig`, `shaders/lib/spot.glsl`, shared uniforms in `materials/spot.ts`, settings in `config/lighting.ts`) up and to the viewer's right (the cut side, so it rakes across the cut face), aimed at the head. It follows the camera's turn lazily (about 2.5 s behind, `spot-path.ts`), so shadows slide across him as he's turned, and drifts slowly on its own (not with reduced motion). Its cone and distance falloff leave the robe darkening toward the base. Each view sets how much the spot takes over (`Look.drama`): at 1 the camera-fixed key, fill, sheen and highlights drop to a low base and the ambient term nearly to nothing, while the rim stays (a little stronger) to hold the silhouette. Cut faces take the spot too, with a floor (45%) so the anatomy stays readable; glowing faces are self-lit. The scan view keeps the even lighting (drama 0).

### Idle life

`specimen/idle.ts` holds the specimen's idle life as pure functions of time, so everything that needs it agrees: each glowing part breathes on its own rhythm (`uPulse`), the Cor Aethericum beats (a lub-dub every 1.6 s), the Crystalline Eyes' glow blinks out for a moment every few seconds (`uGlowLevel`), and everything above the brim sways, more toward the tip. The sway is a vertex offset (`shaders/lib/sway.glsl`) with shared uniforms that `Specimen` updates each frame; the section stencils get it through `materials/sway.ts` (injected into their built-in material), and `LabelTracker` offsets anchors the same way, so cut faces and leader lines stay attached. Pulses of mana leave the Aetheric Core at random moments (one in most 4 s slots, at a random point in it) and travel outward slowly: parts in `MANA_CARRIERS` (core, lattice, vessels, ganglia) brighten where a front passes (`manaWave` in the surface and cap shaders, by distance from the core, fading with it). `specimen/Motes` adds about 60 faint motes (`MoteMaterial`, additive points in the view's `moteColour`) in a loose shell around the specimen, drifting slowly up and fading in and out (all in the vertex shader), to give depth as the camera moves. Sway, blinks and the pulses are off, and the motes hold still, for visitors who prefer reduced motion.

### Gaze

The irises and pupils are separate left and right parts that carry a `gazePivot` (the eyeball's centre) in the manifest. `specimen/gaze.ts` (`GazeRig`) computes one rotation shared by both eyes, so they always move together: toward a point on the pointer's ray, 60% of the way from the camera to the point between the eyes, softly limited to about 0.8 rad from straight ahead (so the eyes keep responding near the limit rather than freezing) and smoothed. The pointer is tracked over the whole page (labels included); when it leaves the window, the eyes ease back to straight ahead. `specimen/GazePivot` applies the rotation about the pivot, in the specimen and to the parts' section-cap stencils, so cut faces stay correct.

## Ornament

`src/ornament/futhark.ts` is the plate's one alphabet: the 24 Elder Futhark runes as straight strokes on a 4 × 8 grid, a Latin transliteration, and helpers that draw a rune as SVG path data or onto a canvas. Every rune on the plate comes from it.

`scene/RuneCircles` draws rune rings (`ornament/rune-ring.ts`: circles, bands of transliterated Latin, dial ticks, a valknut) onto canvas textures: a brighter summoning circle on the ground just under the bust (outer and inner rings turning opposite ways, fast enough to read against the camera's idle turn) and a faint ring that always stands well behind the head (clear of the hair) facing the camera. They're drawn additively (the ground circle in the view's mana colour, just past the bloom threshold; the halo faint, in the marginalia's pale chalk), and hold still with reduced motion. The ground circle is a pedestal: a depth-only disc just under it hides what's behind (the halo's lower part, and the marginalia, which are drawn only where the depth buffer is empty).

The bust stands **in** the ground circle, as if rising through it with the rest of him below (its base sits just under the circle's plane, `scene/ground.ts`). Two lights in the circle's colour sell it, both past white so they bloom:

- a **seam** on the body itself (`shaders/lib/seam.glsl`, shared uniforms in `materials/seam.ts`): a band on every part's surface and cut face, with a bright core pinned at the ground and a softer band above it;
- a **ground glow** (`specimen/GroundGlow`, `shaders/ground-glow.*.glsl`): a bright edge around his footprint and a soft falloff outward across the circle, fading by its rim. The footprint is the outer layer's meshes sliced just above their lowest point (`specimen/ground-slice.ts`; the manifest's bounds are padded, so the real base comes from the vertices), turned at load into a signed distance field (`specimen/footprint.ts`: rasterised outline, flood-filled outside, exact distance transform) in a small texture. The shader intersects it with the kept side of the sectioning plane (the larger of the two distances), so the light wraps whatever is there, cut face included. It hides with the outer layer.

Both undulate together (`pwUndulate` in `seam.glsl`): three waves travelling round the base at different counts and speeds, their phases pushed about by slow noise so they never loop. The waves only change how far the light reaches (the band's height, the glow's spread) and its brightness, so the edges always glow. They share one clock (`uSeamTime`), held still for reduced motion.

The **border** (`ui/PlateBorder`) is a solid black frame (`--frame`, everything outside the inner rule; the rules are opaque so nothing shows through them) with a double rule whose band carries a line of runes (Latin about the specimen, transliterated) placed to fit the window, with a medallion (ring, diamond and rune) at each corner; every second or so a random rune lights up in the mana colour and fades (not with reduced motion). On small screens it's a plain double rule.

The **inset study** (`ui/GlowKey`, top right) is the key of lights: each magical organ's plate number and Latin name beside a swatch in the colour it glows in the current view (from `partColours`), hovering a row flaring that organ, with a small ruled scale. The label overlay starts its right-hand column below it; it hides on small screens.

**Cipher notes** (`ui/CipherNote`) are short Latin notes written in runes that cross-fade to the Latin on hover or focus (screen readers get the Latin): one under the taxonomy, one at the foot of the key of lights.

**Marginalia** are drawn into the paper by the paper effect (`shaders/marginalia.glsl`), only where the depth buffer is empty, so they sit in the page behind the specimen, centred on the specimen with fresh camera matrices so they don't trail it: a faint astrolabe centred on the specimen's projected centre (rings and degree ticks turning very slowly one way, twelve spokes the other) inside a zodiac band of twelve houses, tilted planetary orbits with planets creeping round, great arcs sweeping out to the frame, constellations of linked stars in the margins, compass arcs and dashed construction lines, all anti-aliased, in the view's `chalk` at its `marginalia` strength.

## Sectioning

### State

`src/state/plate-store.ts` (zustand) holds the slice: axis, current `offset`, `targetOffset`, travel `range` and whether the handle is active, plus `viewResets` (a counter the camera rig watches). Components subscribe to what they display; per-frame code uses `getState()` so easing never re-renders React.

### Plane and clipping

`slicing/clip-plane.tsx` provides one `ClipPlane` (a three.js `Plane` with `setNormal` / `setOffset`) through context. Every specimen material lists it in `clippingPlanes`; the renderer has `localClippingEnabled`. Fragments are kept where `dot(normal, p) + offset > 0`.

Axes are in `src/config/slice.ts`. Each normal points away from the default camera, so the removed half is the one nearest the viewer and the cut face looks toward them:

| Axis       | Normal | Removes                            | Resting offset                                                 |
| ---------- | ------ | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sagittal   | −x     | The wizard's left (viewer's right) | −0.012 (just past the septum, so the lateral nasal wall shows) |
| Coronal    | −z     | The front                          | 0.18                                                           |
| Transverse | −y     | The top                            | 0.1 (eye level)                                                |

`slicing/SliceController` recomputes the travel range from the specimen's bounds when the axis changes (`plane-math.offsetRange`), eases `offset` toward `targetOffset` each frame, writes the plane, binds `S`, `[` and `]`, and runs the intro: the plane starts parked just outside the specimen and slides to the resting cut after 1.4 s (set immediately under reduced motion). Changing axis parks the plane and eases it in again.

### Section caps

`slicing/SectionCaps` fills cut faces with the stencil technique, per part:

1. The part's geometry is drawn once, double-sided, with colour and depth writes off and the clip plane on; every surface crossing behind the plane inverts the stencil. Where the plane cuts the part's interior, an odd number of crossings leaves it non-zero. Counting parity rather than back faces minus front faces makes caps immune to the few badly wound triangles simplification leaves, which otherwise drew specks (most visible with the plane parked between the camera and the specimen).
2. A large quad on the plane draws with `CapMaterial` only where the stencil is non-zero, then resets it to 0.

Each frame, a part's stencil and cap are drawn only if its layer is solid and the plane crosses a sphere around it (`plane-math.partSphere` / `sphereSide`: its bounds, grown for the sway, centred on the pivot for the eyes); a part wholly on the removed side isn't drawn at all (`Specimen`). With the cut parked, that halves the draw calls.

Parts are capped in manifest order with `LessEqual` depth, so later caps win where volumes overlap; `specimenParts()` therefore lists parts from outermost to innermost. The effect composer's buffers have a stencil attachment for this.

`CapMaterial` (`rendering/shaders/cap.*.glsl`) paints a deeper version of the part's wash plus its section pattern from the part's look: `hatch` (45° lines; flesh, robe), `stipple` (jittered dots; hair), `rings` (growth rings around a vertical axis; the hat) or `glow` (self-lit; eyes). Pattern coordinates are on the plane, so patterns don't swim as the plane moves. Lines are fine and anti-aliased from their screen-space derivative, fading to an even tone where they'd be closer than a pixel.

### Controls

The scroll wheel moves the cut: `SliceController` listens for wheel events on the canvas and moves the target offset by `SLICE.wheelStep` per 100 px (scrolling down pushes the cut deeper), eased like every other change. Ctrl/⌘ + wheel is left to the camera rig, which zooms with it; trackpad pinches arrive as Ctrl + wheel, so they zoom too. camera-controls' own wheel handling is switched off. On touch screens, two fingers dragged up or down move the cut (`SLICE.touchStep` per 100 px), while camera-controls handles only the pinch (`TOUCH_DOLLY`). There is no on-screen handle.

`ui/SliceControls`, bottom right: the section's Latin name (click to change axis) and a reset mark (camera and cut).

## Labels

### Registry

`src/anatomy/registry.ts` holds the nineteen features (text mirrors [anatomy.md](anatomy.md)): number, name, Latin name, lore, the manifest part it belongs to, and two lists of candidate anchors in specimen space:

- `section`: points to label where the cut passes through the part.
- `surface`: guesses near the part's surface, for features seen uncut. Internal features have them too (on all sides, or along their trunks), so they label from outside whenever the layers over them are ghosted or hidden.

### Tracking (in the scene)

`labels/LabelTracker` runs every frame inside the Canvas:

1. **Section anchors.** If the cut is in (not parked) and the camera is on the removed side, each candidate is projected onto the plane and kept if the part's SDF is negative there (`anchors.sectionAnchor`). The fields come from `specimenParts()`, the same pure code the bake uses, so labels sit exactly on the cut face and follow it as the plane moves.
2. **Surface anchors.** Snapped onto the part's surface at start-up (Newton steps on the field). Every 6 frames each is checked (staggered, so a sixth of them per frame): it must face the camera; if the cut is in, it must be on the kept side and the sight line must not cross the plane inside an outer part (that would be looking through a cap); and no kept geometry may be in the way, tested with `three-mesh-bvh` raycasts against every part whose layer is shown (ghosted and hidden layers don't occlude). The BVHs are built one part at a time after start-up to avoid a stall.
3. Visible anchors are projected to CSS pixels (after refreshing the camera's matrices, which the camera controls leave stale until render, so leader lines don't trail the model by a frame) and written to a `LabelChannel` (`setSample`, `setViewport`), which notifies its subscriber.

### Overlay (in the DOM)

`labels/LabelOverlay` renders one button per feature (number, italic name, and a tooltip with the Latin name and lore) and an SVG of leader paths and anchor dots, once. On each channel update it lays out the visible labels (`layout.layoutLabels`: left or right column by the anchor's side of the specimen's centre, sorted by height, pushed apart to a minimum row height and kept within the column's limits; the left column starts below the title block) and writes positions, paths and visibility straight to the elements, only where they changed (a still camera costs no DOM work). Labels are placed by `transform`, so moving them skips layout.

Leaders are cubic curves from the label to the anchor with a small per-label wobble. Hovering or focusing a label thickens its leader; hover, focus or click shows its tooltip, and a click pins it until Escape or a press elsewhere. Hidden labels are taken out of the tab order and hidden from assistive tech. Below 720 px wide, labels show their number only and the tooltip adds the name, and tooltips open toward the middle of the screen. Labels in the lower 45% of the screen open their tooltip upward. Each column stops short of the bottom controls (further on narrow screens, where they stack higher).

Hovering, focusing or pinning a label sets the store's `highlight` to its part: `Specimen` and `SectionCaps` ease that part's `flare` uniform up (brighter, lit from within in its own colour), and the leader line, dot and tooltip accent take the part's current colour (`partColours`, published by `Specimen`) with a soft glow. Each leader has a dark casing drawn under it, and label text a soft dark shadow, so they read over the model. Tooltips are framed cards (double rule, corner diamonds, an accent bar and the Latin name in the part's colour).

## Layers

`ui/LayerControls` (bottom left) has presets (Omnia, Ossa, Viscera, Arcana; `L` steps through them) and an on/off switch per layer, then one see-through switch (Perspicua, `T`, on at start). Presets only switch layers on and off. The store keeps which layers are on and whether see-through is on, and derives how each layer is drawn (`layerModes` in `src/config/layers.ts`, with the presets): layers that are on are solid; layers that are off are see-through (ghost) when see-through is on, and hidden when it's off. A ghosted part's `InkWashMaterial` blends its colour at a constant opacity while keeping the destination's alpha (the part ids the outline effect reads) and writing no depth, so what's behind it stays visible and inked. Solid glowing parts are drawn after ghosted ones (transparent pass, higher `renderOrder`, no blending), so a see-through shell doesn't dim them and they still bloom. Only shown layers get cut faces or hide surface anchors from view, and a hidden layer's labels go away; so leaving only the arcana shown labels just the arcana, each from outside.

## Camera and input

`scene/CameraRig` wraps drei's `<CameraControls>` (the `camera-controls` library). Settings are in `src/config/camera.ts`. The start (and reset) view is a three-quarter angle at a set distance from a point just above the head's centre (`CAMERA.view`, `framingPosition`); the vertical field of view keeps the bust the same share of the screen's height, and windows narrower than 3:2 start further back so it still fits across.

| Input                              | Action                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Left-drag                          | Orbit                                                      |
| Right-drag, Shift + left-drag      | Pan (target kept inside a box around the bust)             |
| Middle-drag, pinch, Ctrl/⌘ + wheel | Zoom (distance clamped)                                    |
| Arrow keys                         | Orbit in steps                                             |
| `+` / `-`                          | Zoom in steps                                              |
| `R`, double-click, reset mark      | Reset the view and ease the cut back to its resting offset |
| Wheel                              | Move the cut (down: deeper)                                |
| Two-finger drag (touch)            | Move the cut (up: deeper); pinch zooms, one finger orbits  |
| `[` / `]`                          | Move the cut in steps                                      |
| `S`, section name                  | Change the section axis                                    |

The polar angle is clamped so the camera can look down on the hat but not up from under the shoulders. After 20 s without input the camera turns slowly (a full turn takes about two minutes); this is off when `prefers-reduced-motion` is set (`hooks/use-prefers-reduced-motion`).

**Touch.** The plate has `touch-action: none`, so the browser never pans or zooms the page or cancels a drag: one finger orbits, a pinch zooms, two fingers dragged move the cut, and taps still click. On touch screens (`pointer: coarse`) the bottom controls get taller tap targets (`--control-row` in `global.css` sets how far stacked rows sit apart) and number-only labels are widened. Below 720 px wide the cut control moves up a row and the views above it, clear of the layer presets.

## Palette

Defined twice and kept in step: CSS custom properties in `src/styles/tokens.css`, and `PALETTE` in `src/config/palette.ts` for shaders. Washes are Deyrolle-style pigments on ink-black paper, kept fairly deep and saturated (pale pastels wash out against the dark ground); cut faces paint a deeper version of each part's wash, so they follow. The magical organs glow at full strength, each in its own hue (cyan mana in the lattice, vessels and eyes; violet core, rose heart, lime ganglia, magenta cochlea, gold gland, sapphire gems, emerald sinus, warm-white lexicon, ember beard roots), and keep those colours in every view; garnet is for the robe's gems. The page is lettered in chalk (`--ink`).

### Views

`src/config/looks.ts` defines the two views visitors switch between with `ui/ViewToggle` (bottom right, `V`):

- **Tabula** (`nocturne`, the default): `PALETTE`, with blue-black hair, beard, moustache and eyebrows (per-part washes), dark outlines and dark section lines on cut faces (pale lines read as noise).
- **Resonantia** (`mri`): a T2-weighted MRI. Grey by tissue (fluid brightest, muscle and bone dark), the outside dimmed so the cut faces carry the image; hair, hat and robe are blackened (no signal) through per-part washes. Only the magical organs keep their colours.

Each view has a palette, optional per-part washes, outline and section-line colours and strengths, optional surface brightness per layer, and a paper texture strength. The view lives in the store. `Specimen`, `SectionCaps` and `PlateEffects` push it into their materials' and effects' uniforms (`paint(...)`), so switching needs no rebuild. `ViewToggle` sets `data-look` on the root, and `[data-look=…]` blocks in `tokens.css` restyle the DOM chrome.

### Post effects

Both views get these post effects (tunables in `src/config/fx.ts`):

- **Bloom:** arcane parts (surface glow and glowing caps) emit past white in their own hue. The composer's half-float buffers keep the excess, and a threshold bloom after the paper composite turns only them into halos.
- **Grade** (`GradeEffect`, `shaders/grade.frag.glsl`, tunables in `FX.grade`): halation (a warm fringe around the brightest things, from a widened sample of the bloom's blurred texture, mostly where the image is dark), split-toning (shadows slightly cool, highlights slightly warm), a gentle S-curve in the mid-tones and a soft shoulder so glows roll off instead of clipping. Each view sets how much split-toning and halation it gets (`Look.grade`); the scan gets the tone curve only. In dev builds `G` toggles the grade, to compare.
- **Lens:** slight chromatic aberration, light grain and a vignette on the finished image.

**Ink-in.** Once the specimen has drawn its first frame, it inks in over 1.8 s (0.4 s with reduced motion; `FX.reveal`): a ragged front soaks outward from its centre, slightly darker at the wet edge (`shaders/lib/reveal.glsl`). The outline pass applies the mask, so bloom (which reads that pass's output) and the paper only see what has inked in, and glows never run ahead of the wash; the paper shows its marginalia wherever the ink hasn't reached. The labels fade in after it.

Stack order: outline, SMAA (smooths jaggies on silhouettes and ink lines), paper, bloom, chromatic aberration, grade, grain, vignette. The bloom effect is made in `PlateEffects` (not with `<Bloom>`) so the grade can read its texture. The composer doesn't multisample: SMAA alone looks the same, and a multisampled depth-stencil buffer cost about 30 ms a frame at 2500×1330 on Chrome/Windows (ANGLE resolves it slowly), against about 3 ms for the whole frame without it. The outline effect is flagged as a convolution so it always leads its own pass.

### Resolution

The canvas renders at the screen's pixel ratio, capped at 2. `scene/AdaptiveResolution` lowers it in quarter steps (down to 0.75) when drei's `PerformanceMonitor` reports the frame rate sagging, and raises it again with headroom; after three changes of direction it only steps down, so a device on the edge doesn't keep resizing the buffers. Limits are in `config/render.ts` (`RESOLUTION`).
